## pop
Pop music features polished production, accessible song structures, and wide
emotional range. It excels at capturing everyday emotions — joy, heartbreak,
excitement — through catchy hooks and dynamic arrangements. High-energy pop
(energy 0.7–1.0) suits workouts and parties; mid-tempo pop (energy 0.4–0.6)
works for commutes and social gatherings. Listeners expect melodic vocals,
strong rhythmic drive, and a produced, radio-ready sound.

## lofi
Lo-fi (low-fidelity) music is defined by its deliberately imperfect aesthetic:
vinyl crackle, ambient room noise, soft drum loops with minimal variation. It
creates a sense of comfortable solitude — ideal for studying, reading, or quiet
focus. Energy levels are consistently low (0.2–0.5) and tempo rarely surprises
(60–90 BPM). The genre's appeal is its predictability; it stays in the
background without demanding attention, making it the go-to recommendation for
focus and relaxation requests.

## rock
Rock music is built around electric guitars, driving rhythms, and an emphasis
on live-band energy. It spans a wide emotional range — from anthemic and
triumphant to dark and brooding — but always carries a sense of physical
presence and momentum. High-energy rock (0.7–1.0, tempo 130–160 BPM) suits
intense workouts or road trips; mid-energy rock suits focus or emotional
processing. The genre is defined by its sense of authenticity and power.

## r&b
R&B (rhythm and blues) blends soulful vocals with groove-oriented production.
Its hallmarks are expressive singing, smooth chord progressions, and an
emotional focus on love, longing, sensuality, and vulnerability. Energy levels
are typically moderate: slow R&B sits between 0.3 and 0.5, contemporary R&B
reaches 0.6–0.75. The genre rewards attentive listening and suits intimate,
late-night, or emotionally reflective moods.

## metal
Metal prioritises intensity above all else: downtuned guitars, double-bass
drumming, and confrontational energy. Tempos are fast (150–180 BPM) and energy
is consistently high (0.85–1.0). Metal suits high-exertion physical activity
or emotional catharsis. It is the clearest match for "intense" mood and the
highest energy requests in the catalog. Listeners expect aggression, power, and
technical precision.

## indie
Indie music favours authentic, boutique production over commercial polish. It
values emotional honesty, literary lyrics, and sonic experimentation. Energy
and tempo vary widely — indie can be quiet and introspective or loud and
anthemic. Energy typically ranges from 0.3 to 0.8. The common thread is a
sense of personality and independence from mainstream formulas. Suits listeners
who want something that feels personal and genuine.

## jazz
Jazz is characterised by improvisation, syncopated rhythms, and rich harmonic
language. It ranges from ambient background music (quiet piano trios, bossa
nova at 0.2–0.4 energy) to high-energy big band. Coffee-shop jazz sits between
0.3 and 0.5 energy. The genre rewards passive listening as much as active
attention and suits relaxed social settings, late-night introspection, or quiet
work sessions.

## electronic
Electronic music spans calm ambient textures to high-energy dance floor
bangers. Sub-genres differ sharply: ambient sits at 0.1–0.35 energy, synthwave
at 0.6–0.8, EDM at 0.85–1.0. Production is synthesiser-driven with minimal
acoustic elements. High-energy electronic (EDM) suits parties and exercise;
lower-energy electronic (ambient, synthwave) suits focus or late-night moods.
Tempo and energy are the dominant differentiators within this genre family.

## hip-hop
Hip-hop is built on sampled loops, boom-bap or trap drum patterns, and
rhythmically driven vocal delivery. Energy varies widely by sub-genre: chill
hip-hop sits at 0.4–0.65, trap at 0.7–0.85. The genre is versatile — it suits
both laid-back background listening and high-energy sessions. Lyrical content
and flow are central to the experience, making it a strong match for both
"chill" and "intense" mood requests depending on the specific track.

## ambient
Ambient music prioritises texture and atmosphere over melody or rhythm. Energy
is consistently very low (0.1–0.35), tempo is slow or absent, and the goal is
immersive background sound rather than active listening. Suits meditation,
sleep, focus, or any context where the listener wants sound that occupies space
without demanding attention. The purest form of background music.

## classical
Classical music spans centuries of composition styles, but in this context
refers to orchestral or solo instrumental pieces. Energy is low to moderate
(0.1–0.5), tempo varies by piece, and the focus is on melodic and harmonic
development. Suits introspective, romantic, or focused moods. High acousticness
and valence are typical. The genre rewards attentive listening but also works
as refined background music.

## synthwave
Synthwave is a retro-futuristic electronic genre built on 1980s synthesiser
aesthetics: pulsing arpeggios, gated reverb drums, and neon-soaked atmosphere.
Energy sits between 0.6 and 0.85, tempo typically 100–130 BPM. Mood is usually
moody, nostalgic, or cinematic. Suits late-night drives, focused creative work,
or any request with a "dark" or "atmospheric" edge.

## edm
EDM (Electronic Dance Music) is engineered for maximum floor energy: heavy
basslines, four-on-the-floor kick drums, and euphoric build-drop structures.
Energy is 0.85–1.0, tempo 125–145 BPM. The genre is almost exclusively matched
to euphoric, happy, or intense moods and suits parties, pre-workout hype, or
any high-energy request. It is the highest-energy genre in the catalog.

## country
Country music centres on storytelling, acoustic instrumentation (guitar, fiddle,
pedal steel), and themes of home, nostalgia, and rural life. Energy is
moderate (0.3–0.65), tempo is relaxed to mid (85–115 BPM). Mood skews toward
nostalgic, happy, or relaxed. High acousticness is characteristic. Suits
listeners who want something warm, unpretentious, and emotionally grounded.
